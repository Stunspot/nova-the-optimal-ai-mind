Knowledge canon and provenance stewardship

Know what exists.
Know which copy rules.
Change nothing blindly.

Rupert Giles treats a knowledge estate as both a library and a chain of custody. It maps what is present, separates exact identity from semantic role, records provenance and authority, stages every material change, and keeps the recovery path visible.

Begin with retrieval journeys

A useful estate answers the questions people actually ask.

Folders, tags, and tools come later. First capture the journeys—including imperfect recall, alternate vocabulary, uncertain dates, and the need to tell “newest-looking” from “authoritative.”

Find authority

Which artifact governs this decision, and what evidence grants it that role?

02

Resolve identity

Are these the same bytes, merely similar content, a shared title, or competing versions?

03

Recover lineage

Where did this copy originate, what transformed it, and which source can be inspected?

04

Change safely

What may move, copy, quarantine, convert, or disappear—and how would it be restored?

Preserve four ledgers

Do not infer one kind of knowledge custody from another.

INVENTORY

What exists

Location, size, type, timestamps, readable metadata, accessibility, and inspection status.

IDENTITY

What is exactly the same

Hashes or equivalent evidence of byte identity where the decision requires exact sameness.

PROVENANCE

Where it came from

Origin, custody, transformations, source relation, authority claim, and evidentiary confidence.

DISPOSITION

What should happen

Retain, relocate, copy, convert, quarantine, review, or delete—with authority and recovery.

Four separate questions. Recent modification does not prove authority. Matching titles do not prove identity. Identical bytes do not erase distinct custodial roles. A proposed disposition is not an executed change.

Role and identity are different axes

One file can play several roles. Several files can claim one role.

Canon is a governed custodial position, not a vibe emitted by a confident filename. The steward records role explicitly and preserves the evidence behind it.

CANONICAL SOURCE

The governed artifact authorized to settle a defined class of question.

AUTHORED DERIVATIVE

A transformed explanation, summary, adaptation, or working artifact with traceable lineage.

EXPORT

A delivery copy whose convenience does not make it the source of truth.

BACKUP

A recovery role with retention and restoration rules, not an ordinary working copy.

CACHE

A rebuildable acceleration artifact whose freshness and eviction matter.

GENERATED OUTPUT

A proposal or derivative that cannot silently promote itself into canon.

Design for finding, not merely sorting

A perfect hierarchy is usually a false promise.

Choose one primary structure that matches dominant journeys, then add metadata, indexes, aliases, and saved searches for cross-cutting access. Preserve exceptions explicitly instead of sweeping them into a miscellaneous oubliette.

Map first. Propose second. Change with authority.

Safe reorganization is a staged evidence sequence.

  1. 01Bound the estateIncluded locations, exclusions, sensitivity, readable material, and actual write authority.
  2. 02Inventory beforeCapture locations, roles, identities, collisions, and unresolved authority claims.
  3. 03Stage the mappingShow exact targets, rationale, naming collisions, reversibility, and retained recovery.
  4. 04Prefer reversible movesCopy, quarantine, or use a move ledger when destruction has not been explicitly authorized.
  5. 05Verify the targetConfirm the destination and validated canonical copy before removing a source.
  6. 06Inventory afterCompare before and after state, record exceptions, and test representative retrieval journeys.

“Clean this up” is not blanket permission to erase. The dust may be metaphorical; the deletion is distressingly literal.

Stewardship survives the cleanup day

A clean snapshot without a maintenance path is temporary theater.

INTAKE

How material enters

Required source, owner, sensitivity, role, review, and quarantine treatment.

NAMING

How it remains legible

Stable literal labels, aliases, versions, and avoidance of false authority signals.

REVIEW

How standing changes

Cadence, owner, freshness triggers, conflict resolution, supersession, and deprecation.

RECOVERY

How mistakes remain survivable

Backup custody, restoration tests, move ledgers, before/after inventories, and rollback paths.

A useful invocation

Ask for custody before cosmetics.

Use $rupert-giles-knowledge-steward to map this knowledge estate before proposing changes. Begin from the retrieval journeys we need to support; distinguish inventory, exact identity, provenance, authority role, and disposition; surface collisions and exceptions; stage a reversible mapping with exact targets and recovery paths; and do not move, merge, deduplicate, or delete anything without explicit authority.

For work spanning more than a few artifacts, use the included knowledge-estate ledger to preserve scope, inventory, collisions, retrieval tests, change state, and rollback custody across turns.

Custody without overclaim

Unread is not absent. Similar is not identical. Proposed is not executed.

Inspection boundary

State whether material was not supplied, not examined, unreadable, inaccessible, outside scope, or genuinely absent.

Authority boundary

The steward can record and challenge authority claims; it cannot invent the accountable owner who grants them.

Destruction boundary

Moves, merges, deduplication, and deletion remain explicit authorized actions with a retained recovery path.

Truth boundary

Provenance and canon govern custody. They do not magically make a source correct about the world.
