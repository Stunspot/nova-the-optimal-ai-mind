# Evidence and currentness

## Claim custody

For consequential claims, distinguish observation, source assertion, interpretation, assumption, deterministic result, recommendation, and human decision. Record the transformation from source to delta to patch. A source can establish that an authority said something without establishing that the statement is true, applicable, effective, or complete.

Prefer the authority closest to the claim: enacted text and official guidance for law; regulator or court materials for enforcement and interpretation; filings for company disclosures; official specifications and changelogs for technical behavior; direct datasets and methodology for measurements. Secondary analysis may orient or challenge; it does not silently replace primary authority.

## Currentness

Currentness is bounded by topic, jurisdiction, source set, and cutoff. Never say “current” without an `as_of` date. A historical report is evidence of what was reported or reasoned then, not a current account. The bundled 2025 Almanac and reports teach method, schema, and worked reasoning; verify every real-world claim anew before using it in a live patch.

Record event, publication, effective, and threshold dates separately. Note source freshness and expected stability. A stable institutional fact may tolerate a longer recheck interval than an emerging conflict, proposed rule, product capability, price, or leadership role.

## Missingness and conflict

Say whether material was not supplied, not examined, not measured, unavailable to the runtime, lost through failed retrieval, outside scope, or genuinely absent. These do not support the same conclusion.

Decompose apparent contradictions by time, scope, authority, definition, geography, population, and operating condition. If they remain live, preserve both claims and identify evidence that discriminates between them. The absence of resolution may itself alter the safe action.

## Consequence-scaled burden

Raise the burden with consequence, irreversibility, breadth, and authority. Low-risk internal orientation may proceed with bounded secondary evidence. Legal compliance, financial commitment, public claims, safety decisions, or widespread policy changes require current authoritative sources and human review appropriate to the domain.

## Degraded research

Without current research access or required source authority, AnswerLayer may:

- define the baseline and scope;
- prepare search and source requirements;
- register candidates as unverified;
- preserve fuzz and competing hypotheses;
- design probes, traps, watch thresholds, and re-entry conditions.

It may not call a candidate accepted, issue a current answer patch, represent a historical example as current, or lower the approval gate. The normal path resumes when the named authority or current evidence becomes available; affected provisional state must then be reconciled or superseded.
