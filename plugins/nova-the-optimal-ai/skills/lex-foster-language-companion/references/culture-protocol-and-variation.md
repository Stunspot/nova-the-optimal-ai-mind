# Social meaning lives in a distribution

Protocol is coordinated expectation, not a list of national commandments. Language choices emerge from place, community, generation, institution, relationship, identity, medium, and moment. Teach the pattern while leaving room for the person.

## Separate five kinds of claim

- **grammar:** forms conventionally licensed by the language or variety;
- **genre convention:** what a workplace email, wedding toast, group chat, or service encounter usually does;
- **community tendency:** a pattern common enough to prepare for but not universal;
- **relationship choice:** what this pair or group has negotiated;
- **personal preference:** what one person asks to be called, how direct they like requests, which identity terms they use.

Confusing these turns helpful orientation into stereotype.

## Interpret the social variables

When wording changes interpersonal effect, consider:

- power: who can impose cost or grant access;
- distance: stranger, acquaintance, colleague, friend, intimate;
- imposition: how much the request asks of the listener;
- channel: face-to-face, chat, email, public notice, official record;
- urgency: whether mitigation helps or obstructs;
- identity and belonging: which forms include, mark, or distance;
- repairability: how easily a misstep can be clarified.

Explain the cue and likely effect rather than declaring a universal rule.

## Teach options with consequences

Give the learner a small contrast:

- **warmer:** signals solidarity, may sound presumptuous in a distant relationship;
- **more deferential:** protects distance, may sound cold among close peers;
- **direct and urgent:** prioritizes action, may feel abrupt outside an emergency;
- **locally ordinary:** fits the named community, may travel poorly elsewhere.

Let the learner choose the relationship they intend to perform.

## Make variation visible

Name the variety when it matters: region, country, community, diaspora, profession, generation, platform, or signed/spoken/written mode. If the active model cannot support the requested variety confidently, say which aspect is uncertain and keep the output reviewable.

Flags are not languages. National borders do not contain all speakers. “Native” is not a synonym for correct, homogeneous, or culturally authoritative.

## Teach repair as protocol

No learner can precompute every social norm. Rehearse resilient repair:

- ask how someone prefers to be addressed;
- notice surprise or hesitation;
- soften or restate without theatrical apology;
- explain intended meaning briefly;
- accept correction and use the preferred form;
- distinguish harm from harmless markedness.

The goal is not flawless mimicry. It is humane, observant participation.
