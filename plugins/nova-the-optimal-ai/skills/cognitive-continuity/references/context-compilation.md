# Compile the Task, Not the Biography

Useful recall is a task-shaped state, assembled under a budget. Build it from nine responsibilities:

```text
identity and role
+ active goals and agenda
+ commitment ledger
+ beliefs and evidence
+ project decisions and rationale
+ relationships and permissions
+ useful procedures
+ known failures and overrides
+ bounded recent episodes
```

## Pass one: deterministic eligibility

Remove wrong-scope, tombstoned, expired, invalid-time, forbidden-sensitivity, and unreachable-source records. Preserve unresolved conflict markers. Reserve space for active commitments, operative permissions and revocations, and high-consequence known failures before general relevance ranking.

## Pass two: semantic utility

Rank eligible records by:

- direct relevance to the requested transformation;
- authority and source entitlement;
- freshness for the state being used;
- obligation or permission consequence;
- ability to prevent a known failure;
- marginal value per token.

Prefer one source-grounded current record over several redundant episodes. Include an episode when local sequence or exact wording matters. Preserve a concise citation to record and source IDs.

## Make omission principled

When the budget is tight, drop low-consequence biography, redundant support, and weakly relevant history before commitments, permissions, current decisions, or known catastrophic failures. State a material omitted category when its absence limits the task.

## A packet is derived, not canonical

Every packet names its task, scope, creation time, budget, compiler mode, selected IDs, unresolved conflicts, capability limits, and expiry. Recompile when the task, authority, or current state changes. Never edit a packet as though it were the memory store.

Without semantic ranking, use status, scope, authority, due date, recency, explicit tags, and a fixed recent episode window. Label the packet `capability-limited` and state that semantic relevance was not exercised.

## Preserve the meaning that governs the next move

Carry effective time, recording time, scope and source authority into the rendered packet, not only the backing record. Show explicit supersession and conflict relationships with the claims they qualify. A correction, a change of circumstances and an unresolved disagreement are different continuations. Reconstruct the relevant sequence when a present-tense answer depends on how the situation evolved; keep the user's expressed attitude attached when losing it would change the interpretation.

Bring the selected records' eligible source episodes ahead of unrelated recent biography when the budget permits. Read omitted neighbors when exact wording, a conditional exception or an unresolved relationship could change the action. Historical support explains the current state; it does not revive prior permissions, retired procedures or superseded goals. Retain all deterministic scope, sensitivity, source-reachability and redaction boundaries during expansion.

Judge a compiled view by the supported answer or next action as well as the facts it retains. A fully cited packet can still invite a wrong present-tense conclusion. On a concrete failure, distinguish missing capture, incorrect reconciliation, missed retrieval, misleading synthesis and poor execution; repair that seam. Count curation and refresh costs alongside retrieval cost. Prefer task-directed maintenance over repeated estate-wide condensation.

This method draws an engineering lesson from Zhou et al., *Filesystem-Based Memory for LLM Agents* (arXiv:2607.26637v1, Appendix D.1): facts may survive transformation while temporal and narrative meaning does not. Its small conversational and procedural experiments do not establish universal superiority of a storage format or months-long reliability.
