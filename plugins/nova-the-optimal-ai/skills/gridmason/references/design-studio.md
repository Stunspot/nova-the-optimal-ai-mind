# Design studio

Design advice becomes valuable when it changes blocks the player can place.

## Read the evidence

From screenshots and references, inspect only what is visible: silhouette, scale cues, foreground and background relationship, massing, roof line, value contrast, palette families, rhythm, focal point, repetition, depth, terrain fit, lighting, and obvious functional constraints.

Distinguish:

- **visible feature** from **invented continuation**;
- **aesthetic reference** from **voxel specification**;
- **rough footprint** from **measured footprint**;
- **palette family** from **exact block availability**;
- **survival-buildable** from **creative or tool-assisted**.

## Translate taste into a build

A useful design brief normally contains:

1. one-sentence concept and the intended player experience;
2. footprint and height, measured or visibly labeled as assumed;
3. front, side, and roof massing;
4. a primary, secondary, accent, and utility palette with substitutions;
5. functional zones and circulation;
6. a stage order that preserves access and avoids rebuilding;
7. a material strategy appropriate to survival or creative play;
8. the next 20–60 minute move;
9. the one image, measurement, or choice that would improve the next revision.

When the player supplies an inventory or material budget, close the material loop. Separate what is already available, what is craftable from those inputs, and what still has to be acquired. Do not smuggle an unlisted requirement into the mandatory build; offer a substitution, a later stage, or an explicit supply gap.

For large builds, establish silhouette and proportions before decoration. For small builds, spend scarce blocks on depth: recesses, overhangs, frames, stairs, slabs, trapdoors, vegetation, and light only where they serve the concept.

Do not bury the player under ten equally weighted variants. Offer at most three when each expresses a real tradeoff, then recommend one.

## Reference translation

Identify what must survive: roof gesture, facade rhythm, verticality, asymmetry, material temperature, monumentality, coziness, or another defining feature. Make Minecraft adaptations explicit where the source uses impossible perspective, curves, unsupported spans, hidden geometry, or texture density below block scale.

Without a measured footprint and player-approved hidden geometry, stop at a design brief. A compelling concept is allowed to be approximate; an exact coordinate plan is not.
