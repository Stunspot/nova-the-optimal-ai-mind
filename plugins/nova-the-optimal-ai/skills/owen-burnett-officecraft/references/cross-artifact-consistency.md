# Keep one packet from becoming several competing truths

Every multi-artifact job needs one place where consequential content is governed. The content ledger carries the items whose drift would change a reader's decision or trust: controlled title, owner, dates, names, metrics, units, definitions, recommendation, risk language, and source status.

## Assign canonical ownership

For each consequential item, identify its origin and the artifact that owns changes to it.

| Item | Canonical owner | Derivative use |
|---|---|---|
| Calculated budget or KPI | Workbook calculation or verified source data | Brief, deck, PDF reproduce the approved value and unit |
| Narrative recommendation | Content spine / approved brief | Deck and handout adapt detail without changing meaning |
| Branded layout | Authorized native template or source artifact | Exports inherit it; derivatives do not redefine it |
| Final fixed presentation | Editable document or deck source | PDF is regenerated after source changes |

Do not repair a stale derivative independently unless the source is intentionally retired and the ledger records a new canonical owner.

## Reconcile by identifiers, then read for meaning

Compare controlled identifiers mechanically where possible: names, dates, versions, units, figures, labels, filenames, and artifact statuses. Then read the packet for semantic drift: a slide can preserve a number yet reverse its implication through an altered denominator or recommendation.

When a mismatch appears, trace it to the earliest authoritative source. Correct there, regenerate affected derivatives, rerun the narrow check, then reread the packet segment whose premise changed. Retain the unresolved mismatch when source authority remains genuinely conflicted.

## Version with useful names

Use names that reveal artifact, audience or purpose, and version/date where useful. Keep originals separate from working copies and outputs. Record each artifact's format, editability, canonical parent, current status, and inspection evidence in the artifact register. Avoid mystery filenames and invisible replacements.

## Make revision a controlled loop

On new evidence or user correction, update the brief or ledger first when it changes a consequential fact or decision. Mark affected artifacts as needing regeneration or reinspection. Preserve a small decision entry for material changes: what changed, why, who supplied or approved it, and which outputs were touched. This creates continuity without turning the Officefile into a transcript.

## Hand off a family, not a pile

The packet receipt should let another person answer: Which file do I edit? Which one do I send? Which one was actually inspected? Which figures govern the rest? What remains open? Cross-artifact consistency is a maintained relationship, not a similarity of filenames.
