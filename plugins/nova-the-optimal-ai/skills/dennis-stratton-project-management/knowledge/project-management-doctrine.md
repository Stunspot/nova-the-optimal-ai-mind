# Project-management doctrine

## Delivery is sensemaking under constraint

A project is an open socio-technical system. Technical architecture, team topology, decision rights, incentives, vendors, resources, risk, adoption, and political behavior jointly determine delivery. Optimize the whole system; local efficiency can reduce global throughput.

Begin with the intended outcome and benefit. Outputs, milestones, and activity matter only as causal steps toward that change. Continue to test the business or mission justification at meaningful boundaries.

## Choose control surfaces, not ceremony

The primary control surfaces are:

- flow: work moves through the system without uncontrolled queues or partial inventory;
- uncertainty: material assumptions and risks are exposed and shaped early;
- integration: components and teams prove the joined system, not merely local completion;
- authority: decisions occur where accountability and resources meet;
- incentives: actors benefit from the project outcome rather than local scorekeeping;
- value: deliverables remain connected to the benefit they are supposed to enable;
- truthfulness: bad news can travel without punishment or cosmetic transformation.

Use an artifact only when it supports a decision, exposes a weak signal, carries state, or enables a handoff. A document's existence is not evidence that its function is alive.

## Define and align

A useful charter makes these items explicit:

- outcome and strategic reason;
- in-scope, out-of-scope, and non-goals;
- constraints and material assumptions;
- project owner and decision rights;
- risk appetite and escalation thresholds;
- completion and evidence contract;
- stakeholder map and operating cadence;
- current hierarchy and first controlled commitment.

Alignment is not everyone nodding at kickoff. Stress-test incompatible expectations, authority without resources, hidden dependencies, unrealistic dates, and benefits that no operational owner accepts.

## Plan by uncertainty and dependency

Planning continuously reduces uncertainty. Decompose scope into product- or outcome-oriented deliverables whose completion can be observed. Avoid work breakdown structures organized only by departments; they hide integration.

Sequence by:

1. existential assumptions and irreversible choices;
2. high-risk technical or organizational interfaces;
3. bottlenecks and long-lead dependencies;
4. value-bearing vertical slices;
5. reversible detail.

Use rolling-wave planning. Detail the near horizon to the level required for coordination and proof; keep distant work at capability or outcome level until evidence narrows the cone of uncertainty.

Estimate with ranges and confidence. Historical throughput and analogous evidence beat optimism. Relative estimates help a stable team plan; they are not universal productivity units. Do not compare teams by story points or convert uncertainty into false decimal places.

## Protect flow

Throughput falls when work in progress, queueing, context switching, and review congestion rise. Full utilization is usually a queue factory. Identify the system bottleneck and protect it; optimizing non-bottlenecks merely creates inventory before the constraint.

During execution:

- cap active work;
- make blocked time and queue age visible;
- integrate early and repeatedly;
- distinguish started from usable;
- remove dependencies or assign owners and service expectations;
- inspect the whole causal slice before broad tests;
- diagnose failure mechanism before repair;
- preserve a small recovery margin rather than scheduling every person at 100%.

Adding people to late, tightly coupled work can make it later through onboarding, communication paths, and disruption. Add capacity only when the bottleneck, absorption cost, and time-to-effect support it.

## Govern uncertainty and risk

Risk is uncertainty affecting objectives. A useful control records the objective affected, cause/event/effect, exposure, owner, treatment, funding or capacity, trigger, due condition, residual state, and escalation threshold.

Distinguish:

- risk: uncertain future exposure;
- assumption: a planning proposition requiring validation;
- issue: a condition already affecting execution;
- dependency: an external or upstream condition the work relies on.

Do not preserve hundreds of generic entries. Focus attention on the constraints capable of changing the outcome. Test critical assumptions early through spikes, prototypes, interface checks, and direct stakeholder evidence.

## Manage decisions and change

Accountability is singular for an outcome even when responsibility is shared. Avoid a RACI that assigns so many participants that no one can decide.

A decision log preserves options, tradeoffs, authority, rationale, date, baseline effect, and supersession. A change request assesses scope, schedule, cost, risk, quality, benefits, dependencies, and authority before approval. Update affected baselines after approval; otherwise change control is a rubber stamp attached to an obsolete plan.

Manage by exception: define thresholds at which the team can continue and thresholds requiring owner or governance action. Escalate early enough that options remain.

## Attend to stakeholders and incentives

Sponsors must provide decisions, resources, and cross-boundary intervention, not merely optimism. Quiet resistance appears as delayed data, missing operational ownership, nominal agreement, and postponed transition work. Diagnose awareness, desire, ability, incentives, and local risk rather than prescribing another communication blast.

In vendor or multi-team work, examine what contracts and performance metrics reward. Milestones paid for documents or component activity can be perfectly achieved while the integrated outcome fails. Align evidence and incentives with usable system behavior and adoption where authority permits.

Aggressive imposed deadlines can reduce truthfulness. Heavy reporting can reduce delivery capacity. More meetings can increase communication entropy. Design governance to improve decisions and flow, not to display control.

## Monitor predictively

A healthy status system connects leading signals to decisions:

- integration evidence and defect escape;
- cycle time, queue age, blocked time, and WIP;
- dependency health and schedule float;
- decision latency;
- assumption validation;
- scope/change load;
- forecast ranges and confidence movement;
- adoption or benefit readiness;
- team capacity and truthfulness.

Treat all-green reporting in a high-uncertainty project as a reason to inspect, not celebrate blindly. Ask for system-level proof and the worst credible disconfirming evidence.

## Close for continuity

Closure confirms the agreed outputs and handoffs, reconciles cost/schedule/scope evidence, records residual risks and operational debt, releases resources, and transfers benefit ownership. Capture lessons as changed rules, checks, designs, or reusable knowledge. “Communicate better” is not a lesson; it is an aspiration wearing a nametag.
