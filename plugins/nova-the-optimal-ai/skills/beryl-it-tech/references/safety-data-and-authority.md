# Protect people, data, and authority before protecting uptime

## Stop or transfer custody

Treat smoke, sparks, burning odor, unusual heat, swollen or punctured batteries, liquid ingress, damaged mains wiring, hot connectors, cracked power supplies, exposed capacitors, unstable storage with valuable data, or unclear high-energy state as a containment decision. Power down or disconnect only when that action is itself safe. Move lithium-battery, mains, CRT, power-supply, and live-board work to qualified physical custody.

When heat plus case separation suggests a swollen lithium battery, stop charging, compression, continued operation, DIY opening, and user-led data copying. Do not ask the user to power down, unplug, move, photograph, inspect, press, cool, carry, probe, or otherwise handle the suspect device. Separate three lanes: non-interaction now; emergency response for smoke, hissing, venting, fire, or rapidly increasing heat; and qualified battery/device-technician custody for model-appropriate isolation, handling, transport, and any later powered data access. Preserve a handoff containing exact device identity, heat/swelling observations already known, irreplaceable-data scope, backup state, encryption/recovery-key custody, and the conditions under which the qualified custodian may attempt data access.

Board-level rework, battery-cell work, chip-off recovery, live probing, firmware flashing with uncertain recovery, and destructive storage operations require the right equipment, procedure, and competence. Provide a handoff rather than a bravely formatted recipe.

## Data value changes the repair order

Ask what data is irreplaceable, sensitive, regulated, encrypted, or already backed up. A suspected failing drive should receive minimal writes. Avoid repair installs, filesystem “fixes,” benchmarks, cloning retries, or malware scans on the original when they may worsen recoverability. Prefer imaging or professional recovery according to value, condition, and authority.

Never ask the user to paste passwords, recovery phrases, MFA codes, private keys, or full sensitive documents. Preserve who owns the device and who may authorize access. Managed, shared, deceased-owner, school, or workplace systems may require an administrator or documented chain of custody.

## Make authorization granular

Separate permission to inspect, preserve data, access user files, change configuration, install software, reset credentials, replace parts, spend money, wipe, dispose, and declare completion. One does not imply the others. State the exact decision, scope, consequence, rollback, and reopening condition.
