# Worked coaching contrasts

All speakers, employers and responses in this guide are authored fictional teaching examples. They are not an executed model evaluation or observed learner performance.

## A five-minute start

User: “Operations coordinator interview tomorrow. Ten minutes. I ramble.”

Useful continuation: “Let's try one scheduling question. Tell me about a time a plan changed at the last minute and what you personally did.” Wait. A novice should not have to invent a question ID or complete a source ledger first. After the answer, distinguish excessive setup from a missing decision; the user's word “ramble” is a hypothesis, not the diagnosis.

## Coached improvement is not independent transfer

Initial response: “Two instructors were out. I called people, moved rooms and updated a sheet.”

Feedback: the response names activity but not the decision rule or confirmation. Ask what the candidate actually owned. If the manager set priority, preserve that boundary.

Coached revision: “My manager prioritized the registered groups. I checked each instructor's availability, kept one change log and asked each owner to confirm their revised room. I checked the three sessions ran.”

This now shows the candidate's method and bounded result. It does not prove they independently chose a priority rule. A drill targeting personal communication ownership can improve; a drill claiming autonomous prioritization is still unresolved.

Neutral follow-up: “The room owner says the change was never agreed, but your sheet says it was. What would you do next?”

Weak transfer: “I would update the sheet faster.” Same broad coordination topic, missing the actual confirmation mechanism. Keep that gap open.

Stronger proposed method: “I would compare the last confirmed message with the sheet and ask the room owner to verify the current constraint before promising the room. Then I would record the agreed owner and time and send the corrected plan for confirmation.” This demonstrates the intended confirmation behavior on this hypothetical probe. It is a proposed method, not another historical accomplishment.

## No direct platform experience

“I haven't used that facilities system. In our shared log I tracked owners, due dates and unresolved dependencies. I would learn the team's statuses and escalation rules, try a low-risk test request and have someone check the workflow before using it on live issues.” Useful adjacent evidence plus a proposed learning method beats a fabricated certification.

## A source conflict

The resume says “reduced delays by 30%”; the candidate cannot reconstruct the measurement. Ask for the denominator, period and personal contribution. Until clarified, use “made changed bookings easier to track with a single log,” if that is supported. A confident delivery cannot repair an unsupported fact.

## Legitimate alternatives and boundaries

A portfolio candidate explains why they rejected a clever feature to reduce maintenance cost. Judge the actual criteria and trade-off, not whether the story contains STAR labels. A user declines recorded audio; conduct text practice and narrow feedback to what text shows. A recruiter asks for bank credentials; stop that disclosure, retain the message privately and continue benign interview rehearsal while the user verifies the channel.
