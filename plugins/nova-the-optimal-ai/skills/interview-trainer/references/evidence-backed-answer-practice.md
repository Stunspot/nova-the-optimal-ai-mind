# Make the claim no larger than its evidence

Build a few flexible examples from work, study, volunteering, caregiving or projects the candidate can honestly discuss. Paid employment is not the only relevant experience. Ask for the situation, personal responsibility, chosen action, reason, alternative, result and basis for that result. Missing evidence is a prompt for clarification or an honest narrower answer, not an invitation to invent numbers.

For each consequential claim, keep the supporting passage or a dated record of what the user confirms. Distinguish a resume assertion from an independent work record and from a retrospective recollection. Multiple documents repeating the same assertion are not automatically independent evidence. A title, project name or matching topic alone does not support a result, ownership claim or tool proficiency.

## Repair a tempting exaggeration

User material: “I maintained the change log; my manager decided which program moved. All three sessions ran after the team changed rooms.”

Overclaim: “I led the crisis, set the priorities and prevented every cancellation.”

Usable answer: “My manager set the priority. I kept one change log, confirmed the revised room and owner with each instructor, and checked that the three sessions ran.” Ask what the candidate personally checked before adding a stronger causal claim. If the resume says a 30% improvement and the later recollection says “I don't know the denominator,” keep the metric unresolved rather than train the favorable version.

## Fit the answer form

STAR or SOAR can organize a past event. For a proposed method, lead with the decision, assumptions, sequence, checks and escalation. For an experience gap, state it directly, name the closest real transferable method, and describe how the candidate would learn or validate the unfamiliar part. This is not a claim of direct experience.

Use short cues the candidate can reconstruct naturally. Preserve their vocabulary and reasoning. A sample response authored by the trainer is a demonstration to critique, never a record of user performance. The compact answer may omit context but must not change attribution, uncertainty or supported result.

When saving a complete packet, `evidence-support.csv` keeps claim-specific excerpts and rationale; the native helper checks literal custody and joins. An accountable content review still has to ask whether the passage supports the claim and whether anything material was omitted.
