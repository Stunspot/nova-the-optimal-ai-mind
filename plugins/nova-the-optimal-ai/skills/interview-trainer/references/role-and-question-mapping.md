# Role, employer, and question mapping

## Build the employer and role brief

Record the exact role, vacancy source, retrieval date, interview stage, format, known participants, stated evaluation topics, location and schedule, and major responsibilities. Current employer facts require a source and date. Distinguish official employer material, reputable reporting, user-supplied messages, and inference.

The brief should answer:

- What problem is this role hired to solve?
- Which responsibilities and constraints dominate?
- What evidence in the user's background is strongest?
- Which required or preferred areas remain weak?
- What is known about the interview format?
- What is still unknown?

Do not fill unknowns with generic company praise.

## Build question families

Map each likely question to a role criterion and answer evidence.

Useful families include:

- motivation and role understanding;
- relevant accomplishment;
- role-specific method;
- prioritization and ambiguity;
- collaboration and disagreement;
- stakeholder or customer communication;
- trade-offs and judgment;
- error, failure, and recovery;
- learning and unfamiliar tools;
- pace, quality, safety, or compliance;
- leadership or influence where the role requires it;
- logistics and user-owned attestations.

Label probability:

- confirmed: stated by the employer or recruiter;
- strongly-indicated: directly follows from a central role criterion or known stage;
- plausible: common and relevant but not evidenced as planned;
- speculative: possible but low-confidence.

A balanced map is not the largest map. Favor coverage of decisive criteria and material gaps.

## Stage effects

Recruiter or screening stages often emphasize logistics, motivation, communication, and basic fit. Hiring-manager stages often emphasize judgment, role methods, examples, priorities, and team needs. Panel or technical stages may split competencies among interviewers. Final stages may focus on depth, risk, collaboration, and unresolved concerns.

Treat these as planning priors, not employer facts.

## Source register

Preserve vacancy, recruiter messages, candidate materials, official employer pages, and current external sources separately. Retrieved employer facts may become stale. Never present an inference about culture, stability, growth, or interviewer intent as established fact.
