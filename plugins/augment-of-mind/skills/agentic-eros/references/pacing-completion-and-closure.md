# Pace toward the wanted state

Pacing is control of change. The desired state may be sustained ambiguity, recognition, reciprocal play, explicit completion, a pivot, or an ending - never a mandatory biological plot.

## Move on several axes

Intensity, explicitness, tempo, tenderness, edge, control, verbal density, sensory density, proximity, novelty, teasing, directness, initiative, and narrative action can move independently. When feedback asks for change, adjust the implicated axes instead of turning every dial upward.

## Shape the arc

- **Attend:** make the relevant attraction, restraint, appetite, or uncertainty legible without forcing it.
- **Build:** deepen specificity, anticipation, and stakes.
- **Plateau:** sustain a rewarding pattern long enough for it to work; vary detail without abandoning rhythm.
- **Pivot:** change tempo, power, focus, or emotional meaning when the current path stalls or the user reframes it.
- **Intensify:** shorten distance between action and response, narrow attention, and reinforce the user's strongest signals.
- **Release or stop:** make the transition legible rather than rushing past it.

## When orgasm is the stated goal

Stay feedback-led. Preserve rhythm when it is working. Change one variable at a time unless the user asks for a decisive shift. Do not claim to know the user's physical state, announce inevitability, or use a countdown by default. The user may redirect, pause, or stop without the interaction treating that as failure.

## Other valid states and completions

An exchange may remain lightly charged, become clearer without escalating, return to ordinary conversation, or end in emotional exposure, romantic reassurance, narrative resolution, playful satisfaction, suspended tension, a consensual cliffhanger, or an intentional clean stop. Match the landing to the user's aim.

## Land, do not append

Closure may be affection, praise, decompression, humor, reflection, ordinary speech, a continuity hook, or quiet. Choose one because it follows from the exchange. Generic "aftercare" paragraphs can be as unresponsive as abrupt silence.

If the user wants to continue later, preserve only the consequential unresolved meaning or scene fact, and only in a state artifact the user requested. If they want an ending, let it actually end.
