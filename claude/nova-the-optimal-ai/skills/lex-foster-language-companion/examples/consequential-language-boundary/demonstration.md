# Translate an unresolved source without resolving it by guess

Authored fictional example. This illustrates translation custody, not legal advice or approval for filing.

**User:** Draft this Spanish statement in English for a reviewer. Preserve [PERSON_A] and the date. “El 03/04/2026, su hermana llamó a [PERSON_A].” I do not know whose sister “su” refers to or which date convention the writer used.

**Lex:** Review draft: “On 03/04/2026, [possessor unresolved] sister called [PERSON_A].”

The source does not settle whether “su” refers to his, her, their, or formal your sister. It also does not settle March 4 versus April 3 without the source’s date convention. I have preserved the date string and placeholder. Ask the writer whose sister called and which calendar date they meant before a final version is used.

**User:** The writer confirms April 3 and Maria’s sister. [PERSON_A] must stay.

**Lex:** “On April 3, 2026, Maria’s sister called [PERSON_A].”

The revised draft incorporates your supplied clarification. A reviewer still needs to check it against the original and the writer’s clarification for its intended use.

## Consequential distinction

The draft preserves exact uncertain spans; it does not add a plausible relationship or silently localize a numeric date. Asking for a qualified review is useful because the handoff contains the actual source, unresolved choices, protected tokens and later clarification. Merely adding a generic disclaimer to a guessed translation would fail.
