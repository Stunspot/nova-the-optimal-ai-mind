# Evidence foundations and update boundaries

These sources informed product design. They are references, not bundled standards, official endorsements, or authority to issue ratings.

## Functional language over topic completion

The ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines 2024 describe language ability through what a person can accomplish across speaking, writing, listening, and reading, with attention to functions and tasks, accuracy, context and content, and text type. ACTFL restricts official ratings to its authorized tests and raters.

Design implication: observe task-bound functional evidence; never issue an official ACTFL rating.

- ACTFL announcement: https://www.actfl.org/news/revised-actfl-proficiency-guidelines-released
- ACTFL use and official-test boundary: https://www.actfl.org/use-of-actfl-proficiency-guidelines-and-issuing-of-official-actfl-tests

The Council of Europe’s CEFR Companion Volume expands the action-oriented framework with mediation, online interaction, and plurilingual/pluricultural competence.

Design implication: treat explanation, reformulation, code-switching, and helping people understand one another as language capability—not as contamination of monolingual perfection.

- Council of Europe companion-volume hub: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions

## Corrective feedback should change the next attempt

Lyster and Saito’s classroom meta-analysis found significant and durable effects for oral corrective feedback across the included studies, with prompts showing larger effects than recasts in that synthesis. Later scholarship emphasizes that effectiveness varies with linguistic target, learner, context, explicitness, timing, and the measure used.

Design implication: use the least explicit feedback likely to elicit repair, supply a direct model when needed, and always reconnect feedback to meaningful production. Avoid one correction policy for every learner and task.

- Lyster, R., & Saito, K. (2010), *Oral Feedback in Classroom SLA: A Meta-Analysis*, Studies in Second Language Acquisition 32(2), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263109990520
- Xu, M., & Zeng, S. (2023), systematic review of corrective-feedback timing, DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1026174

## Translation quality begins with specifications

Multidimensional Quality Metrics treats translation quality evaluation as specification-led analysis of aligned source and target segments. Its public framework distinguishes errors in areas such as accuracy, terminology, linguistic conventions, style, locale, audience, and design/markup, with severity tied to consequence.

Design implication: recover purpose and constraints before judging quality; classify defects analytically; do not let a single fluency impression or back-translation impersonate verification.

- MQM overview: https://themqm.org/introduction-to-tqe/an-overview/
- Mariana, Cox, and Melby (2015), viability study under CC BY 4.0, DOI: https://doi.org/10.26034/cm.jostrans.2015.343

## Currentness

Framework versions, official-use terms, and public URLs can change. Recheck current official sources before making standards, licensing, certification, or endorsement claims. The product’s durable doctrine is task-led learning, local evidence, purpose-fit translation, and proportionate human review.
