# Authorization and benefits

Load this when a project is proposed, reauthorized, materially changed, recovered, paused, cancelled, transitioned, or closed.

## Continued justification

A project remains justified only while the intended benefit is still valuable, the intervention remains viable, exposure is tolerable, and a better alternative has not displaced it. Record:

- the problem or opportunity and observable world-change;
- credible alternatives, including do nothing, defer, reduce, buy, partner, or operate differently;
- viability evidence and what remains unknown;
- expected benefits, disbenefits, cost, exposure, and strategic fit;
- sponsor, accountable owner, benefit owner, and decision authority;
- the next review date or event that reopens the case.

Do not confuse sunk cost, executive enthusiasm, activity, or a previously approved charter with continued justification.

## Benefits contract

Each benefit needs an outcome, measure, baseline, target, owner, evidence method, review horizon, and status. Delivery outputs can enable a benefit; they are not the benefit itself. If a baseline is unknown, say so and schedule measurement rather than inventing one.

At a gate or exception, ask:

1. Is the benefit still worth pursuing?
2. Is this project still the best intervention?
3. What changed in viability, cost, risk, confidence, or alternatives?
4. Who can authorize continuation, re-contracting, pause, or cancellation?
5. What evidence would change the decision?

## Governance by exception

Agree tolerances for scope, forecast, cost, quality, risk, benefit, capacity, and stakeholder readiness. Within tolerance, the delegated owner acts. Outside tolerance, preserve the facts and escalate to the named authority. A gate is a decision with evidence and authority, not a presentation ritual.
