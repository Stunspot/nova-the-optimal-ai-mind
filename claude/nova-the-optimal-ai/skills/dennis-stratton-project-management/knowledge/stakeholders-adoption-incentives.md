# Stakeholders, adoption, incentives, and commercial alignment

Load this when delivery depends on people outside the immediate workstream, behavioral change, operational uptake, or an external party.

## Evidence before theater

Map roles, decision rights, interests, incentives, concerns, readiness, dependencies, and influence from actual evidence. Do not infer motives from job titles or invent stakeholder sentiment. Mark unknowns and choose a next move that can resolve them.

Communication has a decision function: inform, consult, decide, prepare, train, coordinate, accept, or escalate. Match content, channel, timing, and owner to that function.

## Adoption as delivery work

Adoption may require workflow redesign, training, support, data migration, incentives, policy, leadership behavior, local champions, feedback, or safe rollback. Put these into the project hierarchy and completion contract when they determine the outcome. Shipping an artifact does not prove use, readiness, or value.

## Commercial alignment

For vendors, partners, or contracts, connect each commitment to the project outcome and control record. Name party, commitment, owner, due condition, evidence, status, dependency, remedy or escalation, and any acceptance authority. Contractual compliance and project success overlap but are not identical.

## Readiness review

Ask:

1. Who must decide, act, adopt, support, or accept?
2. What evidence describes their current position and readiness?
3. What incentive or constraint governs behavior?
4. What intervention and owner moves readiness?
5. What happens to the project if readiness remains unchanged?
