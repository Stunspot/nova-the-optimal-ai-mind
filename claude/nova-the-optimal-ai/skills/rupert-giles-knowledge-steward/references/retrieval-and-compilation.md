# Retrieval and compilation: preserve what makes the answer true

Find the evidence needed for the user's present question, then preserve the relations that determine its meaning. A well-organized estate can retain every decisive sentence while making the correct conclusion harder to recover.

## Retrieve a sequence when the state has evolved

Start from the relevant owner's read interface or named source. Inspect descriptive results, matching passages, source anchors and version or time cues; expand into neighboring context when an isolated passage leaves a decisive ambiguity. Keep discovery indexes and compiled packets derived from the sources they describe. Cross-estate synthesis carries each source's ownership, sensitivity and access limits with it.

Bring a current assertion together with the prior assertion, correction, revocation or exception needed to interpret it. Distinguish when something applied from when it was recorded. Check scope and contextual authority before choosing the state that governs this task. Recency alone cannot decide whether a statement is current, authoritative or contradictory.

A note saying “I prefer examples before equations for this unfamiliar topic” supports a situated teaching choice. Compiling it as “prefers examples” loses the condition; compiling it as “dislikes equations” invents one. A later request for equations may concern a familiar topic, correct the earlier account or reflect changed preference. Preserve the supported distinction instead of forcing all three possibilities into one timeless profile.

Read expressed motivation, uncertainty and affect where they change interpretation. Keep the person's words and situation available; do not infer a permanent cognitive type or emotional diagnosis. If the source does not settle a material distinction, carry that uncertainty into the answer.

## Keep sources, interpretations and task views distinct

Retain the evidence selected for preservation under its owner's retention rules. Write a summary or explanation as a named derivative with source anchors; keep an authored interpretation revisable. A compact task view should expose what applies now, the relevant exception or transition, and the source needed to inspect it. It need not reproduce a whole archive.

Preserve exact wording when negation, quotation, nuance or an authority boundary depends on it. Preserve relational meaning when facts answer the question only together. A correction should not vanish because it mentions different keywords from the original statement. A revoked permission cannot become active again merely because a highly similar older note ranks first.

Use the host's existing correction, supersession and forgetting operations. Updating a task view does not silently amend canon; preserving an episode does not override a request to forget it. Keep authoritative identities, accounting, permissions and audit history under their owner's transition rules rather than rewriting them as convenient prose.

## Compile experience into usable guidance

When past work earns reuse, articulate the situation that made the procedure applicable, the decision or action that mattered, the observed result and its limits. Preserve enough source evidence to revisit that judgment. Separate a demonstrated outcome from a plausible explanation of why it occurred.

A successful episode is evidence for what happened in those conditions. A reusable method needs the cue that makes it relevant now and the failure conditions that should change the move. A failed episode may teach a constraint without establishing a successful alternative. Keep raw logs available when authorized and useful; give the consuming agent the guidance needed for its actual task rather than assuming that more chronology is always better.

Adapt the compiled view to the consuming model and the difficulty of the task. More explicit procedural support may help one consumer while another benefits from selective raw evidence. Treat that choice as a revisable engineering judgment, not a universal ranking of model strength or a fixed user type.

## Judge the answer, then the economy

For a material compilation or retrieval change, replay the question most likely to expose the lost relationship against the source evidence and the new view. Check whether the answer preserves applicable time, scope, authority and exception with support. Finding the fact, citing a source and producing the right answer are distinct observations. Localize a failure to capture, reconciliation, retrieval, synthesis or execution before changing the system.

Maintain only what the live question or recurring workflow earns. Repair a stale projection, missing alias or broken connection in the affected area. Stop when that route works; whole-estate tidying is not an automatic follow-on task.

Consider construction and updates together with subsequent retrieval, synthesis, latency, storage and recovery or deletion. Compare reading volume and model cost only between answers that meet the same requirement. Keep source owners and existing bounded stores; an inexpensive read does not justify a universal database or conceal costly repeated curation.

## Research bearing

This authored guidance applies mechanisms examined in Zhou et al., [Filesystem-Based Memory for LLM Agents: Organization, Evolution, and Sustainability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26637v1): organization can change retrieval cost while curation can lose contextual relations even when facts remain. Applying those observations to governed knowledge estates and database-backed projections is an engineering inference. The paper does not establish this skill's behavioral reliability or justify replacing existing storage owners. No paper prompts or implementation code are copied here.
