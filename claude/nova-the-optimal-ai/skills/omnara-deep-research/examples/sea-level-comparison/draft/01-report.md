# A local fall and a global rise can coexist

A falling local tide-gauge trend does not, by itself, contradict rising global mean sea level. The quantities differ: NOAA describes local relative sea level against a land reference, while NASA describes an average across the ocean surface. That makes a difference in sign possible without establishing an error in either record. This is a synthesis of definitions, not an analysis of a particular station. [S001][S002]

## What the two charts would need to mean

NOAA identifies land movement and regional ocean variation as influences on local relative measurements. NASA's displayed global series uses satellite observations since 1993; its page notes that the plotted data omit GIA adjustment. A shared label such as sea level therefore does not establish comparable reference, coverage or processing. [S001][S002]

## The next discriminating comparison

My recommended next step is to request the two actual datasets and their metadata. Align their time window and units, identify reference frames and aggregation, and inspect land-motion information and processing notes. Only then ask whether a residual discrepancy needs explanation. The specific fact needed is a comparable measurement definition and record, not another source repeating the global trend. This procedure is an inference from the measurement distinction; it has not been executed here. [S001][S002]

Do not reuse this answer as a future local flood-design threshold. NOAA distinguishes historical trends from modeled projections. This inquiry has no site-specific scenario, planning horizon or design criterion. Those missing inputs concern a different decision. [S001]
