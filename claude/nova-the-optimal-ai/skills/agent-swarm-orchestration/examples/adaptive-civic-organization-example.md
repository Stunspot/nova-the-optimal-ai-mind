# Worked example: an adaptive organization for a civic sanitation question

This example demonstrates organizational reasoning; it is not a factual plan for Rio or any particular community.

## Mission

Prepare decision-ready sanitation intervention options that are technically plausible, institutionally feasible, and responsive to the stated priorities of affected neighborhoods. No agent is authorized to speak *as* residents, approve an intervention, spend money, contact stakeholders, or act externally.

A generic five-person debate is not sufficient. The task contains distributed evidence, several technical transformations, legitimate human standing, value trade-offs, and a likely phase change from discovery to decision to execution.

## Phase 1: evidence ecology

Borrow anthill mechanics. Broadcast narrow evidence needs rather than one totalizing prompt: existing infrastructure and service gaps; public-health evidence; prior community statements and participation records; land and legal constraints; financing and maintenance history; environmental conditions.

Data-local contributors inspect only their authorized sources. They return provenance-bearing evidence marks and unknowns to a request-scoped board. They do not read one another's full returns, infer a community consensus, or overwrite shared truth. The root reconciles names, dates, geography, source authority, contradictions, and missing voices.

This phase compiles mainly to Assemble. It ends when the evidence map is sufficient to form concrete questions and options, or when a named source or actual participation dependency blocks a claim.

## Phase 2: advisory staff

Borrow general-staff mechanics. Create only the functions the accepted evidence now warrants, for example:

- systems engineering transforms infrastructure evidence into feasible intervention families;
- public health maps credible causal pathways and measurement needs;
- operations and maintenance tests who could sustain each option after construction;
- finance estimates lifecycle burdens and funding constraints;
- legal and institutional analysis identifies decision rights and hard constraints;
- participation design turns evidence gaps into questions and methods for real stakeholder engagement.

These are advisory contributions, not departments with line authority. Each uses the same accepted evidence ledger, names assumptions, and produces a staff product shaped for the root. Where independence matters, first passes remain private. A bounded cross-response may ask engineering to answer an operations objection or finance to compare two maintenance models.

The root integrates premises and preserves conflicts. This phase may Assemble independent staff products, then Chain one bounded integration pass.

## Phase 3: deliberative decision surface

Borrow boardroom mechanics without counterfeiting representation. The “seats” are not fictional residents. They are decision-relevant evidence and legitimate inputs: reconciled technical options, documented community priorities with source limits, actual engagement findings when supplied, fiscal consequences, implementation risks, environmental effects, and reserved owner or public decisions.

A named resolver separates factual questions from value choices. Objective or evidentiary conflicts receive a discriminating check. Trade-offs that depend on whose burden is acceptable, which priorities govern, or whether consent is sufficient remain with the authorized human decision process. Minority evidence stays visible when it may expose a hidden burden or common-mode error.

The organization may finish with a decision object even when no intervention is authorized: options, consequences, unresolved evidence, participation needs, and the exact owner of each remaining choice.

## Phase 4: evidence-triggered reform

Suppose the staff discovers that maintenance feasibility depends on informal local service practices absent from the evidence. Do not add a generic “community persona.” Record the gap, release any contribution that has exhausted its value, and recruit or request a legitimate evidence channel: existing field research, an authorized local expert, or actual stakeholder engagement owned by the proper humans.

Suppose instead that two engineering options remain but one has a reversible pilot and a task-native measurement plan. The root may prepare a bounded experiment within existing authority, comparing it with the nearest simpler option under matched criteria. External field execution still waits for authorization.

Every reform names the observation, changed contribution, exposure, state disposition, next regime, budget, and stop condition. Confidence alone does not rewire the organization.

## Phase 5: controlled execution if authorized later

Only after a real decision and authority exist does the organization borrow platoon or bucketline mechanics. Bounded implementation owners receive intent, scope, constraints, and distinct write or action surfaces. Shared plans and public records retain one canonical writer. Typed dependencies form Chains where accepted surveys, designs, permits, procurement, or verification truly precede later work. Independent state checks inspect real outcomes rather than team reports.

## Why this is not one fixed swarm

The organization changes because the work changes:

`evidence ecology → advisory staff → deliberative decision surface → bounded execution`

Those phrases are not new execution regimes. Each phase compiles into Direct, Enlist, Assemble, Chain, or Recover. The organization borrows mechanisms from several forms, rejects their theatrical baggage, and dissolves responsibilities when their causal jobs end.

The decisive safeguards remain constant: one authorized mission; no simulated stakeholder legitimacy; non-expanding authority; attributable evidence; deliberate exposure; one writer per mutable surface; explicit resolver; event-triggered reform; and complete closure.
