# Build a temporary organization, not a cast list

An organization is a causal arrangement of contributions, relations, information, authority, state, tempo, and resolution. Its useful unit is not the persona name or agent count. It is the difference a member and its connections can make to the accepted result.

## Read the work's physics

Before choosing a familiar pattern, notice the properties that constrain organization:

- **Decomposability:** can work be partitioned without one slice changing another's premise?
- **Coupling:** how much shared context, negotiation, or synchronization does correctness require?
- **Uncertainty:** is the unknown mainly missing evidence, missing ideas, disputed interpretation, future contingency, or value conflict?
- **Observability:** can a task-native check discriminate success, or must evidence and judgment remain plural?
- **Reversibility:** can trials and mistakes be contained, rolled back, or compared safely?
- **Tempo:** does the task reward throughput, careful incubation, rapid local decisions, or long-horizon coherence?
- **Stakes and authority:** which actions, representations, and decisions belong to the root, user, worker, or an external human?
- **Information locality:** is evidence centralized, scattered, private, expensive, or accessible only through specialized tools?
- **Error correlation:** are multiple contributors likely to repeat the same mistake because they share framing, evidence, model, or exposure?

These lenses interact. Do not score them into a magic topology number. Use them to expose what the organization must cause or prevent.

## Design contributions by causal need

Start with verbs. A task may need contributions that discover, retrieve, generate, decompose, model, translate, ground, challenge, cross-examine, test, simulate, integrate, decide, forecast, monitor, execute, or verify. Combine, split, rename, or invent functions as the problem demands.

A contribution earns admission through one or more causal differences:

- distinct evidence or data locality;
- distinct tool or execution capability;
- distinct transformation or representation;
- independent error opportunity or deliberately different framing;
- specialist knowledge that changes what can be observed or tested;
- containment of noisy, adversarial, private, or speculative work;
- an objective check, review, or decision right;
- purposeful redundancy for confirmation, resilience, or search coverage.

Purposeful redundancy is not free. State which common failure it guards against, how independence is protected, how duplicate returns resolve, and what budget or stop condition bounds it.

A role name is optional. A vivid persona can make a stance memorable, widen search, or improve interaction. It cannot substitute for a differentiator, evidence burden, authority envelope, consumer, or release condition. Remove the names mentally; if the organization disappears, it was probably costume.

## Sketch several structures when the choice matters

For non-obvious work, compare the strong Direct route with one or more causally different organizations. Do not merely compare three sizes of fan-out. Vary the mechanism: independent search versus staged transformation, advisory synthesis versus local mission command, blind proposals versus bounded cross-response, fixed team versus evidence-triggered recruitment.

Use the Organization Sketch to capture:

- the current reasoning horizon and acceptance;
- the direct or nearest simpler baseline;
- the work physics that matter now;
- the contribution needs and reasons for existence;
- relation, exposure, state, resolver, and verification choices;
- borrowed organizational mechanics and rejected implications;
- transition and stop signals;
- compilation into the next execution regime.

The sketch is a revisable thinking surface. It is not proof that the organization will work, and it is not required for routine Enlist or obvious Assemble work.

## Use analogy as a mechanism library

A useful analogy answers two questions: what mechanics are we stealing, and what implications are we refusing?

**Bucketline** suggests typed adjacent handoffs, standardized interfaces, bottleneck visibility, provenance, and edge-local repair. It does not imply that every task is uniform or that the stages should run before their inputs are accepted.

**Anthill** suggests decentralized local search, broadcasts of needs rather than full context, stigmergic evidence marks, self-selection near data, and central reconciliation. It does not imply global exposure, peer overwrite, permanent workers, or majority truth.

**Platoon** suggests a clear intent, bounded unit ownership, local discretion inside authority, situation reports, reserves, and independent state verification. It does not grant rank-based truth, new authority, or permission for multiple canonical writers.

**Boardroom** suggests chartered viewpoints, independent preparation, explicit conflict, selective challenge, a named chair or decision right, and preserved dissent. It does not require a fixed cast, all-to-all debate, consensus, or simulated stakeholder legitimacy.

**General staff** suggests distinct advisory functions, a shared planning frame, products shaped for one decision owner, and advice without line authority. It does not let specialist consensus issue commands or erase the decision owner's reserved choices.

**Complex adaptive system** suggests heterogeneity, local interaction rules, environmental feedback, recruitment, pruning, and phase transition. It does not justify unbounded emergence, endless rounds, confidence-driven rewiring, or loss of state lineage.

The same method can mine a court, newsroom, studio, scientific community, market, orchestra, expedition, emergency room, incident command system, neighborhood assembly, or an invented metaphor. No list is complete. Prefer the composition that expresses the causal needs over the name that sounds most prestigious.

## Model stakeholders without counterfeiting them

Agents may assemble attributable evidence about what affected groups, customers, residents, officials, experts, or operators have said and done. Label source scope, time, selection bias, uncertainty, and whether a position is observed, reported, inferred, or hypothetical.

A simulated persona is neither participation nor consent. Do not assign an agent the standing to speak *as* a community or stakeholder unless a real authorized representative owns and reviews that contribution. Let agents prepare issue maps, evidence packets, questions, scenarios, and trade-off surfaces. Preserve actual stakeholder input and value decisions for the humans with legitimate standing.

## Keep design authority honest

The root may configure work within the user's authorized mission. It may not use organization design to create new external actions, risk acceptance, data access, publication rights, or representational legitimacy. A worker may decide within its bounded contribution and still lack authority over the mission, canonical artifact, or user-facing choice.
