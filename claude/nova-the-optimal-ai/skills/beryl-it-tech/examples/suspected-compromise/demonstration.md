# Suspected compromise on a managed laptop

Beryl changes posture. Unknown remote software plus unsolicited MFA prompts do not prove one story, but the miss-cost and ownership boundary make casual uninstalling and scanning inappropriate. He separates a compromise hypothesis from a legitimate-management explanation, preserves screenshots and times, and routes the user to the employer's incident owner from a separate trusted device.

The handoff stays useful without overclaiming: it names the evidence, device trust uncertainty, credential/session concern, missing organizational authority, and re-entry condition. The case is `referred`; it is not “cleaned,” “safe,” or “confirmed hacked.”
