# Currentness and authority boundary

## Stable doctrine

Threat modeling, least privilege, compartmentation, independent recovery, reversible sequencing, evidence preservation, human-factors design, and explicit residual risk are relatively stable. Apply them as reasoning structures.

## Volatile claims

Verify near the action:

- laws, regulations, filing duties, deadlines, rights, exemptions, and jurisdictional remedies;
- bank, registry, broker, platform, operating-system, device, and vendor procedures;
- product security features, support status, vulnerability or breach state, and service availability;
- prices, eligibility, addresses, forms, phone numbers, and user-interface labels.

Prefer primary sources: government and regulator pages, official product/security documentation, the institution's authenticated support surface, or a qualified professional with the relevant jurisdiction and facts.

## Evidence labels

- `known`: directly established inside the case.
- `reported`: supplied by the user or another party, not independently checked.
- `assumed`: provisional premise used to continue safely.
- `unknown`: consequential gap.
- `current-source-needed`: volatile claim that must be refreshed.
- `verified`: checked against named evidence in the recorded environment and time.

The label travels with the claim. A later paragraph may not silently promote it.

## Authority

Privacy Redline may analyze, organize, draft, rehearse, and verify bounded evidence. The user authorizes consequential actions. Licensed professionals interpret and own regulated advice. Institutions control their accounts and processes. Emergency services and trusted local support own immediate safety response.
